Early-stage hiring is full of impressive-looking profiles that do not match the work. The resume is polished. The brands are strong. The title sounds right. Then the person joins and struggles with the actual mess of an early company.

Screening well means comparing the candidate to the role as it really exists, not the neat version in the job description.

## Define the job behind the title

Before screening, write the real job in plain language.

Ask:

- What will this person own in the first 30 days?
- What messy work can they not avoid?
- Which decisions will they make without much help?
- What would failure look like?
- Which skills are nice to have but not essential?

If the role is unclear, every strong profile will look tempting.

## Look for evidence, not just nouns

Candidates list tools, industries, and outcomes. You need evidence of behavior.

Instead of overvaluing:

- "owned growth"
- "worked cross-functionally"
- "built dashboards"
- "managed stakeholders"

Look for:

- what they personally shipped
- how much ambiguity they handled
- whether the result is measurable
- what constraints they worked under
- whether the work resembles your reality

A startup needs proof of doing, not just proximity to impressive work.

## Watch for mismatch risk

The best candidate on paper may be wrong for the stage.

Risk signals include:

- only worked with large teams and heavy support
- optimized mature systems but never built from scratch
- needs narrow scope in a role that requires ownership
- talks about strategy but avoids execution detail
- has brand-name experience with unclear personal contribution

None of these are automatic rejections. They are questions to test.

## Screen the profile against the actual role

Use a simple structure:

- strong evidence
- missing evidence
- possible risk
- questions to ask
- verdict

The verdict should be plain: strong fit, possible fit, weak fit, or not enough information.

Avoid vague summaries like "interesting background." They do not help the recruiter or founder decide.

## Use interviews to test the gaps

The screen should shape the interview.

If the gap is execution, ask for a specific example where they personally built something under time pressure. If the gap is ownership, ask about a decision they made without a clean playbook. If the gap is role fit, ask how they would handle the first two weeks in your company.

Do not ask generic questions when the profile already tells you what needs testing.

## Keep notes reusable

Hiring gets messy when every conversation resets context.

Good notes include:

- candidate name
- target role
- strongest evidence
- biggest unanswered question
- recommended next step

That lets the team compare candidates instead of relying on memory and vibes.

## The real goal

Screening is not about being harsh. It is about being honest before both sides waste time.

The right early candidate does not need a perfect resume. They need evidence that they can do the work your company actually has.
